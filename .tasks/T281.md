# T281 — a test suite with DELETE authority on production

## Summary

Found 2026-08-25 while chasing a **stage-0 REGRESSION** the plan checker raised: `just
db-restore-verify` had gone from met (2026-08-21) to exit 1.

`tests/support/hooks.ts:88-96` runs, in `AfterSuite`, against whatever `SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY`
resolves to — which for every `just` recipe here is `--env=beta`, **the live production database**:

```js
await supabase.from("contact_messages").delete().ilike("email", "%test%");
await supabase.from("contact_messages").delete().ilike("email", "%example.com%");
await supabase.from("job_applications").delete().ilike("email", "%test%");
await supabase.from("job_applications").delete().ilike("email", "%example.com%");
```

plus `blueprints`, `posts`, `github_repos`, `projects`, `interview_templates`, and
`auth.admin.deleteUser()` over the first 1000 users.

## 🟢 What was actually lost this time: nothing real

Measured against `backup_20260823_081510.sql`, the 9 missing rows were **all synthetic**:

| table | rows | addresses |
|---|---:|---|
| `job_applications` | 5 | `dup@example.com`, `gauge-test-1787199003951@example.com`, `gauge-test-1787199948665@example.com`, `noname@example.com` |
| `contact_messages` | 4 | `test@example.com` ×4 |

⇒ **No real applicant or contact was destroyed.** That is the finding's *only* good news, and it is
**luck rather than design** — nothing in the mechanism arranged it.

## 🔴 Finding 1 — the pattern matches real people

`ilike '%test%'` is an unanchored substring match on an email address. It matches
`latest@…`, `protest@…`, `contested@…`, `greatest@…`, `testa@…`, and **any domain containing those
letters**. `job_applications` holds **real job applicants** — live currently contains
`m.adil2018223@gmail.com`, a real person. Had their address contained `test`, their application
would be gone, with no confirmation, no soft-delete and no audit row.

⚠️ **This is the estate's own recorded trap, in a new place:** *`\b` matches across hyphens*, *never
insert by unanchored substring replace* — the same class, now with `DELETE` behind it.

## 🔴 Finding 2 — the restore check fires correctly and explains itself WRONGLY

`db-restore-verify` printed:

> ❌ a table restored MORE rows than live holds — **this dump is not of this database.**

The dump **is** of this database. `restored > live` has two causes and the message names only one:
a wrong dump, **or live having lost rows since the dump**. It was the second.

⇒ A correct alarm with a wrong diagnosis sends the reader hunting a backup-provenance problem while
the actual event is **deletion from production**. And because it surfaced as a `stage-0 REGRESSION`
on the **safety floor**, the wrong explanation was attached to the loudest signal the plan has.

## Acceptance criteria

- [x] AC1 — 🔴 **The E2E teardown must not be able to DELETE from production.** It runs with the
      service role against `--env=beta`. Options, in order of preference: refuse to purge unless the
      target is a local/scratch database; scope every delete to rows the run itself created (the
      suite already has `state.uniqueToken`); or drop the purge entirely and let a local database be
      reset instead. ⚠️ **Whatever is chosen must FAIL LOUDLY when pointed at production**, not skip
      silently — a purge that quietly does nothing is how this would rot back in.
- [x] AC2 — **No unanchored substring match may drive a DELETE, ever.** `%test%` matches real
      addresses. If patterns survive at all they anchor (`gauge-test-%`, `%@example.com` as a full
      domain suffix) — and a probe must demonstrate the anchored form NOT matching `latest@`,
      `protest@`, `contested@`.
- [x] AC3 — **Fix `db-restore-verify`'s diagnosis.** `restored > live` must report BOTH candidate
      causes and, where it can, distinguish them — a dump of a different database differs
      structurally, while post-dump deletion differs only in row counts. 🔑 The check was right to
      fire; only its sentence was wrong, and the sentence is what a reader acts on.
- [x] AC4 — **Recover or accept the 9 rows, explicitly.** They are synthetic and almost certainly
      disposable, but *"we looked and decided"* is different from *"we never looked"*. The dump is
      `backup_20260823_081510.sql` and the addresses are listed above.
- [x] AC5 — **Re-establish the stage-0 exit, and record why it broke.** The safety floor was met on
      2026-08-21 and is now a REGRESSION. It must not be re-marked met by re-running against a newer
      dump until AC1 lands, or the next test run reopens it. ⚠️ **Do not "fix" this by taking a
      fresh backup** — that hides the deletion behind a new baseline.
      ✅ **MET 2026-08-26** — `just db-restore-verify` exit 0 against **the same 08:15 dump**, every
      difference accounted for as post-dump change. Two causes were tangled in one red and are not
      the same problem:
      · `public.deploy_log` 36 vs 37 — **not a loss at all.** That table dates rows with
      `deployed_at`, which was missing from the checker's candidate list, so *our own release* wrote
      a row the checker could not date. Fixed in `wom7api/scripts/db_restore_verify.py`. The refusal
      was correct; the list was incomplete. An unlisted timestamp column turns every routine deploy
      into an alarm on the estate's safety gate.
      · `public.profiles` 25 vs 23 — **a real deletion, and a SECOND one**, distinct from the 9 rows
      this ticket opened on. It happened *after* the 08:15 dump, so unlike those 9 the dump still
      held the rows ⇒ **repairable, and repaired** rather than re-baselined
      (`project/repairs/T281-restore-profiles.sql`, owner-run 2026-08-26). The green therefore sits
      against the **pre-incident** dump — the strong form the DoD below had written off as
      unachievable, which was true of the 9 and not of these 2.
      🔒 Proven durable, not just repaired: the next live E2E run left both rows in place, because
      AC7's snapshot now lists them as pre-existing. Before that fix the same run deleted them.
- [x] AC6 — 🔑 **Audit what else in `tests/` writes to a live database.** `auth.admin.deleteUser()`
      over the first 1000 users is in the same teardown and was not the subject here only because
      its filters happen to be narrow (`gauge-test`, `test@example.com`, `sessiontest@example`).
      Narrow-by-luck is the same defect as `%test%`.
      ✅ **AUDIT RUN 2026-08-25, and it had NOT been when this was first ticked** — the tick covered
      only the identity purge inside the same teardown, not `tests/` as the AC asks. Re-done
      properly: **20 mutating call sites across 4 files** — `hooks.ts` (9, the defect, fixed),
      `admin.steps.ts` (8), `ats.steps.ts` (2), `ai.steps.ts` (1).
      🟢 **Verdict: the teardown was the sole offender.** Every delete in `admin.steps.ts` is a REST
      call filtered `?…=eq.<value>` — **exact match on a value the test itself supplied**
      (`job_applications?email=eq.…`, `projects?name=eq.…`, `blueprints?slug=eq.…`). None is
      unfiltered, none uses a LIKE pattern. `ats.steps.ts` and `ai.steps.ts` only insert/upsert.
      🔑 **The distinction worth keeping: `eq.` is narrow BY DESIGN, `%test%` was narrow BY LUCK.**
      That is the same line the identity filter failed on, and it is the rule to apply to any new
      cleanup code — scope by an identifier the run owns, never by a word that appears in one.
      ⚠️ **One residual, a leak rather than a hazard:** an `eq.` delete only runs if its step is
      reached, so a scenario that fails mid-run leaves its row behind on live. Worth a sweep some
      day; it cannot delete anything it did not create.
- [x] AC7 — 🔴 **On LIVE, an identity may be deleted only if THIS RUN created it.** AC1 was written
      as *reserved domains only*, and that is a weaker property than the teardown's own comment
      claims (*"delete ONLY what this run created"*). The fixtures use **fixed** addresses
      (`dup@`/`noname@example.com`, created by `tests/steps/ai.steps.ts`), so an address cannot
      tell a row this run made from one a run in **May 2026** made.
      📉 **What it cost, 2026-08-25.** The address-only sweep reached live for the first time since
      the TDZ fix revived these hooks, deleted both `profiles` rows, and the paired
      `auth.admin.deleteUser` **failed with its error discarded** — leaving two auth identities
      alive with no profile, while the log printed `Purged 2 orphaned test identities`.
      🔑 Two distinct defects, not one: deleting *by the wrong key*, and *a half-deletion nobody
      logged*. The blunt fix (never delete on live) is the one the DoD below records as considered
      and **rejected** by owner direction, so it was not taken.
      ✅ Delivered: a `@BeforeSuite` snapshot of pre-existing ids; live deletes only ids absent from
      it; **fail-closed** — a null snapshot (hooks silently dead, which is exactly what happened)
      purges nothing and says so; every discarded error in the teardown is now reported.
      🔒 5 assertions in `test_purge_patterns.py` (27/27), mutation-tested — reverting the live
      branch to address-only, or dropping the null check, both go red.
      ⏳ **Residual, owner action:** the 2 rows are still absent from live. The repair is written to
      `project/repairs/T281-restore-profiles.sql`; an agent write to the production database is
      blocked here by design, so a human runs it.

## Definition of Done

Per [`../../openspec/SPEC-AUTHORING-STANDARD.md`](../../openspec/SPEC-AUTHORING-STANDARD.md):
no delete is driven by an unanchored substring, proven by a probe over real-looking addresses; on a
hosted database the teardown deletes **only rows this run created** — a reserved address AND absence
from a pre-run snapshot — **fails closed** when that snapshot is missing, and **reports every
deletion it could not complete**; the mode is chosen from the host rather than a flag; the restore
check names both causes of `restored > live` and can date a row in every table that logs one; the 9
lost rows are explicitly accepted or recovered, **and so are the 2 this ticket's own fix deleted**;
the rest of `tests/` has been audited for live writes; and stage-0's exit carries **why it broke**,
not merely a green.

🔄 **AMENDED 2026-08-26 — one clause was too weak, and this time it WAS a work gap.** Third
revision, recorded rather than silently edited, for the same reason as below.
· **"only at addresses no real person can hold"** — true, and **insufficient**. No real person holds
an `@example.com` address, so the clause was fully satisfied while the teardown deleted two fixtures
created in **May 2026** that this run never made. An address identifies a **namespace, not an
owner**. ⇒ Replaced by the property [[T281]]'s own AC6 had already written down and the code had
never implemented: *scope by an identifier the run owns, never by a word that appears in one*.
· The half-completed deletion added a condition the DoD never carried at all: a teardown that cannot
say what it **failed** to delete is not a safety property. Both are conditions now, not commentary.
· 🔑 **This amendment was forced by a gate, not noticed by a human.** `dod-check` refused the very
commit that added AC7 — *"scope GREW 0.9d after the DoD was last touched"*. That gate is the open
question filed on [[T242]] after two stale DoDs turned up in one day, promoted to blocking
2026-08-25. It worked on its first real encounter, against its own author.

🔄 **REWRITTEN 2026-08-25 — two clauses were false, and neither was a work gap.** Recording that
here rather than quietly editing, because a DoD that changes without saying so is worse than one
that is stale.
· **"the teardown cannot delete from a non-local database"** — **superseded by owner direction
mid-implementation**: *"we still have to preserve possibility to do testing probes on live with
cleanup after for only what was created."* ⇒ The delivered design **does** delete on live, confined
to `@example.com` (RFC-2606 reserved) and `@test.webomage.com`. The original clause described a
blunter rule that was considered and rejected; leaving it would have failed the ticket for building
what was asked.
· **"stage-0's exit is re-established without hiding the deletion behind a fresh baseline"** —
**became unachievable at 08:15 that morning**, when the scheduled backup captured the post-deletion
state. The exit is green AND it sits behind a fresh baseline; nobody chose that, the schedule did.
The achievable form is the one now written: the exit carries the *reason*, so the green is not
mistaken for an absence of incident.
🔴 **Second stale DoD in one day** — [[T267]]'s was found the same morning, there because scope grew.
⇒ Two different causes, one shape: **the DoD certifies the plan, and nothing re-checks it against
the work.** `ticket-schema-check` verifies the section EXISTS. Worth a gate; filed as the open
