# T265 — the tier between "ours" and "not ours"

## Summary

Owner direction 2026-08-24, refining the previous day's correction:

> *"Evercred is also shadow managed project via Elena with own scripts/skills and isolated sandbox
> in Tart VM. So for these 2 we have to test first unmanaged (like client self-managed) projects,
> kinda half managed with skills/tools and sdlc/workflow but without our ticketing system and maybe
> something else."*

⇒ **This is not an exclusion, it is a THIRD TIER.** A half-managed project **gets** our skills,
tools and SDLC/workflow conventions; it **does not get** our ticketing system. **BridgeRoad and
Evercred are the two pilot subjects**, both Elena Shatalova's engagements worked from this machine.

🔄 **Re-scoped twice, and the sequence is the record.** Filed 2026-08-23 as *"Import BridgeRoad as a

is the point made at the bottom of this ticket.

The tier is not being invented; it is being named.

## Two findings that fall out of the measurement

🔴 **1. `~/.tart` is 49 GB and sits OUTSIDE [[T274]]'s audit scope.** That audit scanned `~/Projects`
and reported **155.16 GB** total. The Tart VM storage is not under `~/Projects`, so the machine holds
**~49 GB more** in this category than the report says — a ~32% undercount against its own headline.
⇒ Reported to T274 as AC8's *re-measure before acting* case, and it is exactly the shape T274 AC1
already warns about: **a scan is bounded by its root, and the root is a choice.**

⚠️ **2. `_proj/Evercred/TASKS.md` exists and is NOT a task board.** It is unrelated correspondence
that happens to carry the filename — 36 lines, zero T-ids, no rows. **A sweep globbing for
`TASKS.md` would find it and treat it as a board.** This is CLAUDE.md's standing rule arriving with
a new instance: *a surface is identified by its CONTENT, never its directory name* — the rule
[[T192]] broke four times inside the ticket that established it.
🔴 **I hit it myself while measuring.** My own probe printed *"no TASKS.md"* on the line directly
below the one showing the file exists, because a compound `ls a b || echo` fired its fallback on the
missing second path. **The tooling for this tier must not detect by filename**, and AC5 says so.

## The design questions this pilot has to answer

**What half-managed GETS** — skills, tools, SDLC/workflow conventions. How do they arrive? Via
`just skills-sync`, or does the project keep its own (Evercred is described as having *"own
scripts/skills"*)? [[T127]] requires fully-qualified provenance for anything governed; a project
with its own skills is either outside that or needs a reduced form.

**What it does NOT get** — our ticketing. Stated by the owner, and it settles a live question:
[[T242]] AC9 asks *where a client project's tickets live*, and for this tier the answer is
**not in our system**. No T-ids, no GitHub mirror, no `sync_github.py`.

**What "maybe something else" turns out to mean** — the owner left it open, deliberately.
Candidates the measurement suggests: secrets (Artemio already diverges to dotenv, [[T272]]),
CI/deploy, the debt register, `client-check` gates. 🔑 **AC2 requires each to be decided
explicitly rather than inherited** — a tier defined by what someone forgot to exclude is not a tier.

🔴 **And the substrate question, which is new:** Evercred's real work is inside a **running Tart
VM**. Does any gate reach into a VM? The recorded sandbox direction is that it is deliberately
unmanaged. If governance stops at the VM boundary, the tier's guarantees stop there too, and that
has to be stated rather than discovered.
