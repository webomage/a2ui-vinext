## Summary

Found 2026-08-06 by reviewing the **top 10 of the `sdlc` board** — the board that answers *what is
closest to done*. Three of those ten cannot be finished at all without work owned by a **different,
`queued`, unstarted** ticket. The board presents them as nearly complete.

| Ticket | Board | The AC that cannot be done in place | Owned by |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[T22]] #3 · 2/4 | *"Standing project health"* | **AC4** — functional guardrail wired | **[[T8]]** *(status: queued)* |
| [[T110]] #4 · 2/5 | *"SDLC enforcement"* | **AC3** — the verify gate must actually pass; it cannot, because `pnpm test` is broken | **[[D7]] → [[T104]]** *(queued)* |
| [[T20]] #10 · 2/4 | *"Session poisoning"* | **AC4** — rolling-summary memory, written in the ticket as *"= T7"* | **[[T7]]** *(status: queued)* |

T20's AC4 is the clearest case: the acceptance criterion **is another ticket**, stated as an
equation in the ticket text. Nothing in the data model records that, so `ac_progress()` counts it as
one more open checkbox exactly like a checkbox someone could tick this afternoon.

## What the model supports today, and what it does not

`status:` carries `queued | in-progress | blocked | done | paused`, and `just tasks blocked` lists
`blocked`/`paused` rows (currently 2: T32, T18). So **whole-ticket** blocking exists.

🔴 **There is no `blocked_by` / `depends_on` field anywhere** — verified by grep across all 171
ticket files: zero occurrences. So the queue can say *"this ticket is blocked"* but not
*"AC3 of this ticket is blocked by D7, which T104 owns"*. The three rows above are all the second
kind, which is why none of them is marked blocked — **marking them blocked would be wrong too**,
since most of their remaining ACs are genuinely workable.

The ranker is honest about being a heuristic (`tasks_query.py:21`: *"a ticket blocked on an owner
action scores LOW here even when it matters"*). This is the mirror failure: **partially blocked work
scores HIGH**, and nothing in the recorded fields could have told it otherwise.

## Why it matters more than a ranking nit

- The `sdlc` board exists to answer *"what should I finish"*. For 3 of its top 10 the honest answer
  is *"you cannot"* — and an agent picking up the board has no way to learn that without reading
  three tickets and one debt row.
- It compounds with a real gap: **[[T110]] AC4 records that nothing checks a ticket passed the SDLC
  gates.** So the queue neither enforces the process nor accurately reports readiness.
- It is a measurement trap of the estate's signature shape — **a number computed over a collection
  that was never asked whether its members are comparable.** `ac_progress()` counts checkboxes; it
  never asks whether a checkbox is *ownable by this ticket*.

## Non-goals

- Building a full dependency graph or critical-path scheduler. The need here is *"do not tell me
  something is 2 checkboxes from done when one of them is another ticket"*.
- Changing what `hot` measures. `hot` is deliberately blind to completeness; that is its job.
