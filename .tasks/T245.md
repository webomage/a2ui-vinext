# T245 — Gate the status-vs-acceptance-criteria disagreement

## Summary

Every ticket states its progress **twice**: once as `status:` in frontmatter, once as the ratio of
ticked `AC<n>` bullets. The two are maintained by different things — **the AC count is
tool-maintained and read by the ranker; `status:` is whoever-remembers** — so they drift by
construction. Nothing compares them.

🔴 **Five mismatches were found by hand in two days** — [[T236]], [[T239]], [[T231]], [[T198]] and
[[T243]]. Five is not five careless authors; it is a missing mechanism. [[T198]]'s own status note
already made this argument (*"a gate comparing them beats a fifth manual fix"*) and did not file it;
[[T243]] then became the fifth, sitting at **6/6 = 100% with `status: queued`** for a day, holding
rank 4 on the SDLC board while finished.

🔑 **This is not new machinery — it is completing an asymmetry.** `project/scripts/ac_trace_check.py`
already builds `(id, status, priority, done, total)` for every ticket and already implements **one**
direction: `status: done` with unticked criteria → *stale-done*, a hard failure (line ~97, `_all_stale`).
The inverse comparison uses data already in hand and was simply never asked for. That is why five
instances were found by eye rather than by the tool that had both numbers the whole time.

**Measured 2026-08-21 across 219 measurable tickets:**

| shape | count | reading |
|---|---|---|
| 100% ACs, `status` not `done` | **0** | T243 was the only one; fixed the same day |
| `status: queued`, some ACs ticked | **15** | work started, status never moved — T152, T153, T184, T199, T200, T201, T203, T207, T208, T219, T232, T237 + 3 |
| `status: in-progress`, **0** ACs ticked | **12** | ambiguous — T2, T42, T71, T75, T81, T87, T88, T95, T106, T155, T185, T263 |
| `status: done`, unticked ACs | 1 | T211 — already caught, and legitimately exempt via `closed_as: duplicate` |

⇒ **27 tickets (12%) carry a disagreement nothing detects.** 🔴 **The sharpest instance is [[T2]]** —
top of the HOT board at score 105 and lead of the *blocking* plan stage — reading `in-progress` at
**0/5**. Whatever the truth is, one of those two fields is wrong on the single most important ticket
in the queue.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] **AC1** — **100% ticked ACs with `status` not in `{done}` is an ERROR.** This is the
      unambiguous direction: the ranker says finished, the field says otherwise, and the ticket
      occupies a slot it has no claim to. Exactly [[T243]]'s shape.
- [ ] **AC1b** — 🔬 **AC1's rule as written would MISFIRE, found 2026-08-21 by a live
      counter-example — handle it before shipping the gate.** [[T2]] reached **6/6 ACs while
      correctly reading `status: in-progress`**: its AC6 fix touches `_shared/*`, so it is complete
      in the repo and **not deployed**, and no AC tracks deployment. AC1 would have called that an
      ERROR and demanded the ticket be flipped to `done` — publishing "shipped" for an undeployed
      change, which is a worse lie than the drift it was written to catch.
      ⇒ **100% ACs is not the same claim as done**, because the AC list is not required to cover
      everything closure depends on (deploy, an owner decision, a live verification). Either the
      rule reports rather than errors, or it needs a declared "what remains" escape — but it must
      not assume the AC list is exhaustive. 🔑 Fitting for this ticket: the first draft of a gate
      against the record lying was itself about to encode a falsehood.
- [ ] **AC2** — 🔴 **AC1's corpus is EMPTY today (0 instances), so a gate written now passes
      immediately and proves nothing.** It must be demonstrated against a **fixture** that it
      rejects, and **an empty corpus must not be reported as a pass** — print the denominator it
      searched. This estate has shipped vacuous greens before ([[T240]]; a gate satisfied *by* the
      corruption it should catch, [[T228]] AC11), and T243's own AC6 refused the mirror-image
      inversion of widening a check to make a corpus green.
- [ ] **AC3** — `status: queued` with **any** ticked AC is reported. 15 instances. A ticked criterion
      is evidence work happened, and `queued` claims it has not started. ⚠️ Decide the tier
      deliberately: 15 reds landing at once is the shape that trains people to ignore a gate
      ([[T240]]'s lesson). Advisory-with-a-count, or a ratchet that forbids *new* instances, are both
      defensible; a silent pass is not.
- [ ] **AC4** — `status: in-progress` with **0** ticked ACs is reported **as a question, not a
      verdict**, because the two readings are opposite: either the status is optimistic and nothing
      has started, or work is landing and nobody is ticking criteria. 🔴 The second reading is the
      dangerous one — it means the **SDLC ranking is scoring real progress as 0%** and the board is
      wrong about 12 tickets. Do not collapse these into one label.
- [ ] **AC5** — the `closed_as`-based exemption already in `ac_trace_check.py` keeps working:
      `closed_as: duplicate` / `not-planned` legitimately pairs a closed ticket with unticked
      criteria ([[T211]] → [[T209]]). Prove the exemption still fires after the change rather than
      assuming it survived.
- [ ] **AC6** — the new rules live **in `ac_trace_check.py`**, not in a new script. It already holds
      both numbers; a second tool reading the same tickets is a second thing to keep in lockstep.
      ⚠️ `just ac-trace-check` already exits non-zero on stale-done, so the recipe's failure contract
      does not change — only what it can see.
- [ ] **AC7** — registered in `project/test-suites.json` with probes, per [[T240]]: an unregistered
      gate is an invisible one, and [[T243]] AC4 applied that lesson at authoring time rather than
      discovering it later.
- [ ] **AC8** — **triage the 27, do not just light them up.** The gate's value is the standing check;
      the backlog it exposes needs a disposition pass so the first run is not permanently red. 🔑 Start
      with [[T2]] — it is the blocking stage's lead, and its 0/5 makes the plan's stage-1 percentage
      unreadable.
