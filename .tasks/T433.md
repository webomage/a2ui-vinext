# T433 — Bug-bounty programme: w0m disclosure posture (security.txt, safe harbor, diodb) and a human-gated hunting pilot

## Summary

Research (2026-09-23, owner request) on joining bug-bounty programs as researchers, on the catalogs and listings that
show where to hunt, and on w0m's own disclosure posture. Full report and diagram:
[`2026-09-23-t433-research-bug-bounty-participation-and-listings.md`](../.agents/reports/2026-09-23-t433-research-bug-bounty-participation-and-listings.md)
· [`.mmd`](../.agents/reports/2026-09-23-t433-research-bug-bounty-participation-and-listings.mmd).

Findings:
- **Start on HackerOne.** It hosts Cloudflare, Supabase, GitHub, Anthropic and Vercel/Svelte. It also has a *Researcher
  Business Account* that explicitly covers company setups using AI agents; Support has to grant it.
- **Agents may assist; they may not hunt unattended.** HackerOne bans fully autonomous hackbots, and Bugcrowd suspends
  accounts after 10 consecutive invalid AI reports. Platform reputation is the main risk.
- **Open-source bounty money is shrinking.** In 2026 curl ended its bounty, the Internet Bug Bounty was paused, Code4rena
  shut down and GitHub's public tiers were cut. Paid programs from the vendors w0m builds on are the realistic targets.
  Treat this as R&D and reputation, not revenue.
- **Machine-readable listings:** `arkadiyt/bounty-targets-data` (MIT) for scopes and `disclose/diodb` (CC0) for
  safe-harbor status. The terms for Chaos data are unclear.
- **w0m publishes no `security.txt`** (estate search, 2026-09-23). `wom7sec/SECURITY.md` is the only channel and is scoped
  to wom7sec. The planned multi-tier audit (openspec 2026-09-11, task 1.3) would flag w0m's own sites. A paid bounty is not
  recommended at this size.

## Acceptance criteria

- [x] AC1 — `/.well-known/security.txt` (RFC 9116) is live on every public app (web, cp, job, join, demo), with `Contact`,
  `Expires` (≤1 year), `Policy` and `Canonical`. An expiry check fails closed ≥30 days before `Expires`.
- [x] AC2 — An estate-wide security policy covers the public apps, extensions and APIs (not only wom7sec), includes
  disclose.io *full* safe-harbor text (dioterms), and is linked from `security.txt`.
- [x] AC3 — w0m is listed in `disclose/diodb` via a PR (the owner approves the PR before it is opened).
- [x] AC4 — The owner decides on HackerOne's free Essential VDP (adopt or decline, with the reason), recorded in `docs/DECISIONS.md`.
- [x] AC5 — The agent-assisted hunting rules are recorded as an AgDR **before any submission**:
  - agents propose and a named human reproduces by hand and submits;
  - agents hold no platform credentials;
  - scope is re-checked against the platform API at test time;
  - per-program rate limits and required headers (e.g. `X-SUPABASE-VDP`) are in the tooling config and fail closed;
  - scanner output is never a report;
  - the programme pauses if fewer than 50% of the last 10 reports are valid;
  - client assets are never bounty targets.
- [x] AC6 — A `wom7sec` intake command joins bounty-targets-data (MIT) and diodb (CC0) into a ranked JSONL shortlist
  (paid + full safe harbor + stack match). It is read-only, runs no scanning, and has tests.
- [x] AC7 — The owner decides the pilot parameters: the legal entity and jurisdiction that receive payouts (tax form),
  the time budget (proposed: 4h/week × 8 weeks), and ≤3 programs (proposed: Cloudflare, Vercel/Svelte, Supabase VDP).
- [x] AC8 — Go/no-go review at the end of the pilot, with a ledger of hours, valid ratio, payouts and what `wom7sec`
  gained. The review includes a decision on applying for an H1 Researcher Business Account.
