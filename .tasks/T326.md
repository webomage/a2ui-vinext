# T326 — Authenticate outbound mail, and send through the signed sender

## Summary

Measured 2026-09-07 with `dig` against 8.8.8.8:

- **SPF — absent.** No `v=spf1` TXT record on `webomage.com`. The only TXT records are two
  `google-site-verification=` strings.
- **DMARC — absent.** No record at `_dmarc.webomage.com`.
- **DKIM — present, for exactly one selector: `resend._domainkey.webomage.com`.** Provisioned and
  Terraform-managed (see [[T125]], which records the record id).

Then the part that inverts the obvious fix. **Nothing sends through the signed sender.** Grepping
the edge functions (excluding `node_modules`):

- **nodemailer / Gmail SMTP** — `client-register`, `contact-submit`, `admin-invite-candidate`,
  `weekly-digest`, `apply-submit`
- **`RESEND_API_KEY`** — appears only in `apps/cp/dist/server/chunks/credentials_DgTl3aOk.mjs`,
  a build artifact. **No edge function sends via Resend.**

Resend is fully provisioned — `RESEND_API_KEY` (len 36) and `RESEND_FROM_EMAIL` (len 21) are both
set in `beta`, and its DKIM key is live in DNS. So the estate is paying for and hosting an
authenticated mail path while every transactional message goes out over Gmail SMTP with a
`"Webomage" <…@webomage.com>` From header, unsigned, unlisted by SPF, and unpoliced by DMARC.

⚠️ **This is a risk, not a proven breakage — and the distinction is load-bearing.** The one live
registration email (2026-08-24) was **delivered and its link clicked** the next day. Unauthenticated
mail is not blocked; it is discretionary. Gmail-to-Gmail rides the sending domain's reputation, so
the failure mode is a *future* recipient on a stricter tenant silently dropping a client's only way
in. Do not file this as "registration email is broken" — measurement says otherwise.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] AC1 — An SPF record exists on `webomage.com` authorising every sender actually in use,
      verified by `dig TXT webomage.com` against an external resolver. If both Gmail SMTP and Resend
      remain in use, both are listed.
- [ ] AC2 — A DMARC record exists at `_dmarc.webomage.com` with a reporting address that someone
      reads. Starting at `p=none` is fine and expected; the policy chosen is recorded with the
      reason.
- [ ] AC3 — A decision is recorded on the sender split: either transactional mail moves to the
      already-signed Resend sender, or Gmail SMTP stays and gets its own DKIM selector. "Both, by
      accident" stops being the state. `client-register` is named explicitly, since it is the one
      that carries a client's only sign-in link.
- [ ] AC4 — Whatever is chosen in AC3 is applied to all five nodemailer callers, or the ones left
      behind are listed with the reason. No silent partial migration.
- [ ] AC5 — Every DNS record added is authored in the **Terraform stack** ([[T125]]), not clicked
      into a DNS panel, and `terraform plan` is clean afterwards.
- [ ] AC6 — A real message from `client-register` is sent to a mailbox on a strict tenant and its
      headers show `spf=pass` and `dkim=pass`. This is the AC that proves the work; the DNS records
      alone do not.
