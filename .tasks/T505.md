# T505 — where a person's phone, address and identity live

## Summary

Owner (2026-09-30), looking at the Resume Studio header: the private resume should list personal contacts (address, phone), the public one should not; and "we have to extend personal information profile since we're going to do kyc".

**Measured today.** `public.profiles` has no phone, address, birth date or legal name (columns: id, email, full_name, avatar_url, description, seniority, years_experience, resume_url, telegram_*, work_email, auth_user_id, org_unit_id, subject_type). No table holds them either. KYC (T349, done for its MX validators) stores only a status flag and `curp`/`rfc`/`kyc_level`/`verified_at` on `gamification.compliance_profiles`, keyed by the LOGIN (`auth.users.id`), with 0 rows. The phone in `resume.json` lives in a private block of a repo file, which is not a place for per-member data. So the resume and KYC would each have invented their own copy.

## Decision: two records, not one, and not a CV-only copy

| Record | Holds | Who writes | Sensitivity | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **`public.profile_contact`** (this ticket) | phone, postal address (line1, line2, postal code), city, region, country, website / LinkedIn / GitHub / Upwork links, and two verification markers `phone_verified_at`, `address_verified_at` | the member writes the content; ONLY the service role (KYC) writes the markers | PII, owner-only, no admin arm | built, migration written, NOT applied |
| **`public.profile_identity`** (follow-up, with T349's personal half) | legal name as on the ID, date of birth, nationality, CURP, RFC, ID document references, `verified_at`, `kyc_level` | the member submits; the service role verifies and locks | highest: encrypted at rest, retention rule, never shown on any resume | NOT built |

Why two: contact is something a member changes often and shows to third parties; identity is proven once and must not change silently. Different write rules, retention and audience. The resume reads ONLY `profile_contact`, and only the phone and street address on the Private variant.

**The rule that makes KYC trustworthy:** a verification marker never outlives the value it verified. A member cannot write a marker (column grant), and the trigger clears `phone_verified_at` when the phone changes and `address_verified_at` when any address part changes, unless the same statement sets the marker (only the service role can). Proven on a scratch Postgres (forged marker refused on insert and update; unrelated edit keeps the marker; phone change clears only the phone marker; address change clears the address marker; another member reads and edits nothing; anon and no-profile read nothing; no DELETE).

## Definition of Done

- A member's phone and street address exist in exactly one table, and appear on a resume only when the member picks the Private variant.
- A "verified" marker cannot be written by a member and cannot survive a change to the value it verified.
- The same record is what KYC will verify; no second copy of a person's phone or address exists.

## Open questions (recommended answers)

1. **What does the Public variant show for location?** Recommend city and country (as the reference PDF does), never the street address. Weight: recruiters need a region; street address is the risk.
2. **Is the Masked variant also supposed to redact client names?** It does today (T484's cv_public convention). The reference public PDF names employers. Recommend splitting into two switches (hide personal contact / mask client names) if you want a public resume that names employers. Weight: low cost, answers a real mismatch.
3. **Employer order.** The resume sorts employers by relevance to the chosen role, not by date, so a 2017-2024 role can precede a 2024-2025 one. Recommend a third option "order: by relevance / by date", defaulting to by date. Weight: a resume normally reads newest-first.
4. **Seeding your own record.** Recommend you type it in the form (nothing copied from the repo file). The private `resume.json` block stays where it is until T165 retires it.
