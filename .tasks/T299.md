# T299 — a base role plus per-service switches

## Why

[[T265]] AC1/AC2 landed a third role, `half-managed`, and a **fixed IN/OUT list per tier** — all 33
contract keys decided once, for the tier. That works, and it is the wrong shape.

🔑 **A tier ladder cannot express the combination clients actually come in.** One wants ticketing but
not deploys. Another wants us on GitHub but running no CI. A third is fully managed except for
secrets. On a ladder each of those needs a new rung, and the rungs multiply until the enum is a list
of customers. Owner 2026-09-02: *"we have to make 'managed' and 'on-prem' and have options
enabled/disabled like GH manage, tickets manage, deploys manage with controlled ci/cd."*

⇒ **`role` says what a project IS; a `services` map says what w0m DOES for it.** The role supplies
defaults; a project overrides individual switches **with a reason**.

## What "managed" would cover — measured 2026-09-02

The estate's capability families, by `just` recipe count — this is the real menu, not an invented one:

| | | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| **deploy 40** | db 16 | authz 16 | cv 14 |
| sync 13 | skills 11 | client 11 | upwork 10 |
| check 10 | backup 10 | spec 8 | tools 7 |
| tasks 7 | headroom 7 | ticket 6 | state 6 |
| graph 6 | zeroclaw 5 | mcp 5 | e2e 5 |

📌 **`deploy` at 40 recipes is the heaviest thing "managed" can mean** — and the one most obviously
wrong for a client whose infrastructure is not ours. That asymmetry is the argument for switches.

## What already exists (do not rebuild)

- **The switch mechanism, in miniature.** `clients-policy.json → tiers['half-managed']
  .contract_surfaces` already maps 33 keys to `{status, reason}`. This ticket generalises that from
  *one fixed list per tier* to *defaults per role, overridable per project* — it does not invent it.
- **The completeness check** ([[T265]] AC2): `client_inventory` fails on an undecided contract key and
  on a decision pointing at a key the contract no longer has. That property must survive.
- **`tier_reason`** and its `minLength: 40` — the precedent that a governance choice carries its
  justification or it degrades into a status nobody can question.

## Acceptance criteria

- [x] **AC1 — `estate` is renamed `w0m`.** Owner: it references the full scope of integration, and
      "estate" names a location while the other values name a relationship. ⚠️ Mechanical but not
      free: `client_inventory` selects the estate by `role == "estate"` and requires exactly one, and
      `.w0m/sdlc.json` carries `profile`. Find every reader before renaming; a half-renamed enum is
      worse than the old name.
- [x] **AC2 — `role` becomes `w0m | managed | on-prem`, and each supplies DEFAULT switches.** The role
      is the starting point, not the whole answer.
- [x] **AC3 — a `services` map with per-project overrides, each carrying a reason.** At minimum the
      families the owner named: github, tickets, deploys (with CI/CD), plus those measured above.
      🔴 An override without a reason is refused, exactly as `tier_reason` is — otherwise "disabled"
      becomes the second silent default beside `enabled: false`.
- [x] **AC4 — 🔴 absence is a finding, never a default.** A service not named for a project must not
      resolve to on or off by inheritance-with-no-record. This is [[T297]] AC2's rule in a second
      place, and the reason is the same: *"nobody said"* must not become *"someone decided"*.
- [x] **AC5 — the completeness check survives the redesign, and extends to services.** Today it
      guarantees every contract key is decided. It must also guarantee every declared service is
      decided for every project, and fail on a switch naming a service that does not exist.
- [x] **AC6 — [[T265]] AC2's 33 decisions are MIGRATED, not re-derived.** They carry researched
      reasons — why `secrets` is out (a real leak into a client repo), why `disk_cleanup` is out
      (approval is the project owner's), why `quality_gates` is *declared but not enforced*. Losing
      those reasons to a schema change would be the expensive half of this ticket.
- [x] **AC7 — 🔴 name `on-prem` deliberately, or pick a better word.** It names *where infrastructure
      runs*; what is actually being encoded is *how much w0m governs*. **BridgeRoad is cloud-hosted
      and half-managed** — under this name it would be miscategorised on day one. [[T297]] already owns
      *where a project runs* as a separate axis; if these two are conflated, one of them stops meaning
      anything.
- [x] **AC8 — red probes for the shape, not just the schema.** A project declaring a service that does
      not exist; an override with no reason; a role outside the enum; and a project where every
      service is off — which should be legal and *visible*, not silently equivalent to absent.

## Definition of Done

`role` names what a project is — `w0m`, `managed` or `on-prem` — and a `services` map names what w0m
does for it, with the role supplying defaults and any per-project override carrying its reason. A
service unnamed for a project is a finding rather than an inherited default. The completeness check
covers services as well as contract keys, and fails both on an undecided service and on a switch
pointing at one that does not exist. T265 AC2's thirty-three decisions arrive with their reasons
intact. And the word chosen for the self-managed role describes governance rather than hosting, so a
cloud-hosted half-managed client is not miscategorised by its own label.

## Notes, traps and dependencies

- 📌 **Supersedes the SHAPE of [[T265]] AC2, not its content.** That AC is done and its decisions
  stand; this changes where they live and how they are overridden.
- ⚠️ **Do not let `services` become a second contract.** The 33 contract keys describe *what a project
  declares about itself*; services describe *what w0m does to it*. They overlap (`tickets` is both)
  and are not the same axis — a single merged list would make one of them unreadable.
- 🔑 Related axis, deliberately separate: [[T297]] (where a project RUNS) and [[T300]] (which identity
  acts on it). Three questions — what governs it, where it runs, who acts as — that each need their
  own answer.
