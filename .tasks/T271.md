# T271 — use the platform we are already on, deliberately

## Summary

Owner direction 2026-08-24: *"since we are on CF it will be really good to use from their services
and tools all the matching to our features and requests/needs"*.

Filed immediately after [[AgDR-0010]] declined **Kitesurf**, and the two are not in tension: that
was a workload-specific no — every browser job here needs pixel fidelity or a logged-in session,
which Kitesurf removes by design. **Being on a platform and not using what fits is the more
expensive mistake**, and nothing about that evaluation argues against the rest of the catalogue.

**What we use today, measured 2026-08-24 from the wrangler configs, not recalled:**

| | |
|---|---|
| `wom7cfworkers` | `ai`, `browser`, `assets`, cron triggers, observability, source maps |
| `wom7api` | `ai`, `workflows`, `assets`, cron triggers, observability, source maps |
| elsewhere | **R2** ([[T231]] backups; [[T262]] wants it for public assets) · Pages/Workers hosting · Terraform (`just cf-plan`) · the Cloudflare **MCP** server (declared + configured, needs interactive auth) |

**Declared in no wrangler config:** D1, KV, Queues, Vectorize, Hyperdrive, Images, Email Routing,
Analytics Engine, Containers, Turnstile, Pipelines, mTLS, Dispatch Namespaces.

## 🔴 The first axis is COST, and there is a standing decision about it

**[[AgDR-0003]] — stay on free tiers; paid only if genuinely needed.** Several of the interesting
candidates sit behind **Workers Paid**, so a survey that ranks by usefulness and discovers the price
last will produce a wish list.

🔑 **This is not hypothetical — it has already decided one of them.** [[AgDR-0005]] evaluated
**Containers** for running `pg_dump` in the backup chain and rejected them: *"needs Workers Paid —
against AgDR-0003"*. ⚠️ I went looking for that record expecting to overturn it, on the reasoning
that Containers can run arbitrary images and the *"a Worker cannot run pg_dump"* objection therefore
fails. **The record had already considered and priced it.** That is the AgDR format working, and it
is the standard this survey has to meet: **check what has already been decided before proposing it
again.**

## 🔑 The comparison the owner named, and it is the better frame

Owner, same day: *"we can measure the possible payments with paid features and compare to the price
of supabase paid service since we can have also more simple backups there naturally."*

⇒ **The question is not "can we stay free". It is "if we spend, where does the money buy the most".**
That is a materially better frame than the one this ticket was filed with, and it reaches further
than the Cloudflare catalogue.

| | what the spend unlocks | what TOIL it removes |
|---|---|---|
| **Workers Paid** (~$5/mo) | Containers, Hyperdrive, Queues, higher limits | Would reopen [[AgDR-0005]] — backup production could leave this laptop |
| **Supabase Pro** (~$25/mo) | PITR, managed daily backups, larger DB, more egress | 🔑 **Would remove most of the home-grown backup chain outright** — [[T229]], [[T231]], `just db-backup`, the restore-verify recipes, and the standing "one laptop" exposure |

🔴 **This is a live re-opening of [[AgDR-0003]]** (*stay on the Supabase Free tier; Pro only if
genuinely needed*). That decision is not wrong — it is a decision **with a stated trade-off**, and
the owner is asking whether the trade-off still holds now that the free tier's cost is visible as
*work we do by hand*. ⚠️ **If the answer changes it, supersede AgDR-0003 with a new AgDR** rather
than quietly acting against it; that field is the reason the format was adopted.

⚠️ **Facts already recorded that this comparison must NOT re-derive from scratch — and must
re-measure rather than trust** ([[T229]], measured 2026-08-18):

- backups are **proven to restore**, so the home-grown chain works — the case for Pro is toil and
  single-machine risk, **not** brokenness;
- 🔴 a dump does **not** carry the `postgres` role, and **pgvector tables do not restore at all** —
  so *"Pro gives us backups"* must be checked against **what PITR actually covers**, or we would be
  buying a different gap;
- `solar_flares` is **284 MB, ~57% of the cap** — headroom is a real input to the sizing half;
- **egress is unmeasurable** on the current plan, which means the egress column of this comparison
  cannot be filled from our own data — say so rather than estimating;
- the estate has **two orgs, the second empty**, so a *third project* needs that org, **not** Pro —
  one previously plausible reason to upgrade is already answered.

## 🔬 Hyperdrive — surveyed and measured 2026-09-21

Covers **AC1** (price re-read from Cloudflare's own pages), **AC2** (paired to a real need), **AC3**
(decision record checked first) and **AC7** (tested, not reasoned) *for this one candidate*. The
other rows in the table above remain unmeasured — this does not tick those ACs.

### Price, re-read from Cloudflare (AC1)

**Hyperdrive itself is free on both Workers plans.** No per-query, per-GB or subscription charge;
pooling, caching and data transfer are all included. The constraint is a quota, not a bill:

| | Workers **Free** | Workers Paid |
|---|---|---|
| Hyperdrive queries | **100,000 / day** (resets 00:00 UTC; cached queries still count) | unlimited |
| Max origin connections | ~20 | ~100 |
| Configs per account | 10 | 25 |
| Max query duration / cached response | 60s / 50MB | 60s / 50MB |

⇒ **No [[AgDR-0003]] conflict on price.** The estate is on Workers Free, so the live limit is the
**100k queries/day ceiling** — a hard stop, not a degradation, which is the same "fails abruptly
rather than gradually" property AgDR-0003 was written about. 439 call sites across 5 public apps
plus the Workflow host could plausibly reach it.

### 🔴 The IPv6 question — raised, tested, and CLOSED (AC7)

It was an open question whether Hyperdrive could reach our database at all, because the estate's
direct connection is IPv6-only and the Supabase IPv4 add-on is **Pro and above**. If the answer had
been no, the whole candidate would have died on infrastructure. **It was tested rather than
reasoned about, and the answer is that IPv6 is not a blocker.**

**Measured, 2026-09-21:**

```
db.pqbbjgeapylcengbuvee.supabase.co
  A     -> none
  AAAA  -> 2600:1f16:1e8d:b801:e0e4:a3c8:f444:fff8      # IPv6-only, confirmed
  TCP :5432            CONNECTED (138 ms)
  Postgres SSLRequest  -> b'S'                          # TLS supported; clears HD error 2012
```

Credential-free pre-auth handshake — no login attempted, nothing mutated.

🔑 **The probe egressed from inside Cloudflare's own network.** WARP was connected, so this
machine's v6 egress was `2a09:bac5:4c09:2da5::48c:3c` — a Cloudflare WARP range. The successful
IPv6 connection was therefore routed **from Cloudflare-operated network space to our Supabase
origin**, which kills the "Cloudflare cannot route v6 to it" hypothesis. ⚠️ Stated honestly: WARP
egress is **not** Hyperdrive egress — different infrastructure, different ranges. It removes the
routing objection; it is not a Hyperdrive-specific proof.

**Cloudflare's own docs agree.** Searching the full 565KB Hyperdrive docs corpus surfaced exactly
one substantive IPv6 statement, in the Fly.io guide, and it points the opposite way from the worry:
it instructs `fly ips allocate-v6` and notes *"Cloudflare recommends using IPv6"* — the IPv4
fallback is framed as being for **your ISP**, not for Hyperdrive. The connection-error table gates
on **public vs private**, not v4 vs v6: error `2009` reads *"does not resolve to a public IP
address"* with RFC1918 IPv4 given only as examples.

**An IPv4 fallback exists regardless** — the Supavisor shared pooler answers on IPv4 with TLS:

```
aws-0-us-east-1.pooler.supabase.com:6543  (transaction)  CONNECTED 309ms  TLS=b'S'
aws-0-us-east-1.pooler.supabase.com:5432  (session)      CONNECTED 302ms  TLS=b'S'
```

⇒ **No Supabase Pro upgrade, no IPv4 add-on, no AgDR-0003 supersession needed to try this.**

**Residual:** only a real `wrangler hyperdrive create` is conclusive — it runs a live test connection
and returns error codes `2008`-`2016`. That needs the DB password via `infisical run` and writes a
config to the Cloudflare account, so it is left for a human to run.

### What actually blocks it: architecture, not infrastructure (AC2)

Hyperdrive is a **Workers binding speaking the Postgres wire protocol**. supabase-js speaks
**HTTPS to PostgREST**. These are not two speeds of one road — Hyperdrive cannot accelerate a
supabase-js call, only replace it. Measured on the CF side:

| surface | `.from()` call sites to rewrite as SQL |
|---|---|
| `wom7web/apps/*` (web, cp, job, join, demo) | **387** |
| `wom7api/src` (Hono worker, Workflow host) | **52** |
| **total** | **439** |

And these supabase-js features have **no Hyperdrive equivalent at all** — they keep their own client
regardless, so the end state is *both* transports, not one: `.auth.*` **124** · `.rpc()` **41** ·
`.functions.invoke()` **15** · realtime/`.channel()` **14** · `.storage.*` **7**.

🔴 **A large share of those `.from()` calls are in browser-side Svelte components**
(`apps/join/src/components/WalletPanel.svelte`, `apps/cp/src/components/admin/*.svelte`, ...) using
the anon key plus the user's JWT. **A browser cannot use a Workers binding.** Those can never move,
at any effort level. This is structural, not a matter of work.

🔴 **The RLS consequence is the one that matters here.** With supabase-js + anon key + user JWT,
PostgREST sets the role and claims per request — enforcement is structural. Through Hyperdrive,
**the caller** must open a transaction and `set_config('request.jwt.claims', ..., true)` on every
query, exactly as `wom7api/supabase/functions/_shared/database/db.ts:34` already does. Miss it once
and the query runs with no policy applied. Converting 439 RLS-enforced call sites to a model where
enforcement is the caller's responsibility, across **98 RLS-covered tables**, is a
**security-posture change, not a performance optimisation**, and must not be filed as one.
⚠️ This estate has already been bitten by the adjacent trap — `SET LOCAL ROLE` outside a
transaction is a no-op — and by [[T39]]'s possession-based admin policies.

### Against T262's actual goal — the hypothesis fails

The candidate row paired Hyperdrive to T262's *"the public site must keep rendering when Supabase is
unavailable"*. **It does not serve that need.** Cloudflare: *"Hyperdrive does not purge or
invalidate cached read query results when your application writes to your database"* — default
`max_age` **60s**, `stale_while_revalidate` **15s**, maximum TTL **1 hour**, and queries touching
`NOW()`/`RANDOM()`/`CURRENT_TIMESTAMP` are never cached at all. A 60-second window is not an outage
story, and the absence of write-invalidation is actively dangerous on the tables we would most want
cached (auth, hats/roles, permissions, quota) given the hat-switching model in [[T117]]/[[T200]].
****KV** is the right candidate for T262**; Hyperdrive is a latency candidate, not a resilience one.

### Where it would genuinely pay, if anywhere

Two narrow slices, both worth measuring before assuming Hyperdrive is the cause of any gain:

1. **N+1 collapse** — PostgREST forces multi-table reads into several round-trips. One SQL join
   replaces them with one. If a CP dashboard is doing 4-6 sequential `.from()` calls per view, that
   is a real latency win — **but a better PostgREST query or an `.rpc()` fixes the same thing with
   zero new infrastructure and zero RLS change.** Establish which it is first; it is the cheapest
   possible outcome and it is plausible.
2. **Cache-safe public reads** on `apps/web` / `apps/job`, where 60s staleness is acceptable.

🔑 Tooling cost for a spike is near zero: `wom7api/package.json` already carries
**`postgres@3.4.9` + `drizzle-orm@0.45.2`** and `_shared/database/schema.ts` already models the
schema in Drizzle. (The edge functions already use direct PG — but they run on Supabase's own infra,
so Hyperdrive, a Workers binding, is irrelevant to them.)

### Proposed verdict for AC5

**REVISIT-WHEN-X, not adopt.** X = a measurement showing CP dashboard latency is dominated by
PostgREST round-trips *and* that an `.rpc()` does not fix it. Hyperdrive is cheap, reachable and
free — and still the wrong tool for the need it was paired to. Re-pair it to latency, hand T262 to
KV, and do not open the RLS rewrite without a measured reason.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] AC1 — **Every candidate carries its free/paid status, re-read from Cloudflare's own pricing on
      a stated date.** 🔴 Anything requiring Workers Paid is measured against [[AgDR-0003]] and needs
      an explicit owner decision, not an engineering one. A survey that omits price is a wish list.
- [ ] AC2 — 🔑 **Every candidate is paired to a NEED WE HAVE, with a ticket or a measurement behind
      it.** A service with no named need is recorded as *"available, no use"* and dropped. **This AC
      is the whole ticket** — the failure mode is adopting a good service for a problem we do not
      have, and being on the platform makes that easier, not harder.
- [ ] AC3 — **Check the decision record BEFORE proposing anything.** [[AgDR-0005]] already priced
      Containers and said no. Re-proposing a settled question as a discovery wastes the record.
      Where a decision is genuinely overturnable, supersede it properly with an AgDR rather than
      quietly disagreeing.
- [ ] AC4 — **Measure what is deployed, not what is installed.** Specifically Turnstile: a
      `turnstile-spin` skill exists locally and proves nothing about production. Same distinction as
      [[T154]]'s *declared ≠ configured ≠ listening ≠ connected*.
- [ ] AC5 — **A written verdict per service** — adopt / reject / revisit-when-X — in `docs/`, with
      rejections carrying their reason. An explicit *"no, because…"* is a complete outcome.
- [ ] AC6 — **Adoptions become their own tickets, scoped from the verdict.** This ticket surveys and
      decides; it does not grow into an implementation. ⚠️ Where a candidate serves an existing
      ticket's goal (Hyperdrive/KV → [[T262]]), the finding goes to **that** ticket rather than
      spawning a rival.
- [ ] AC7 — 🔴 **Resilience claims are tested, not reasoned.** [[T262]] exists because the public
      site should survive Supabase being down. Any candidate adopted for that reason must be proven
      against an actual simulated outage — a caching layer that has never been observed serving
      during a failure is an assumption with a bill attached.
- [ ] AC9 — 🔑 **Price BOTH vendors against each other, not just Cloudflare's catalogue.** Workers
      Paid vs Supabase Pro, on the same axes: monthly cost, what it unlocks, and **what hand-run
      work it deletes**. The last column is the one the owner is pointing at — the free tier's real
      price is a backup chain we maintain and a restore that depends on one laptop.
- [ ] AC10 — **Establish what Supabase Pro's backups actually COVER before valuing them.** 🔴
      [[T229]] measured that our own dumps lose the `postgres` role and **all pgvector data**. If
      PITR has its own gaps, *"simpler backups"* buys a different set of holes rather than none —
      and that would be the worst outcome here, because it would look like the problem was solved.
- [ ] AC11 — **If the verdict changes [[AgDR-0003]], SUPERSEDE it with a new AgDR**, citing this
      comparison. Do not act against a recorded decision without overturning it on the record; the
      `superseded_by` field is the field the format was adopted for.
- [ ] AC8 — **The result is re-measurable.** Record the current binding inventory as a recipe or a
      committed measurement, so *"what CF services do we use"* is answerable next quarter without
      re-reading every wrangler config by hand.
