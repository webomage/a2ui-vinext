## 🔴 Why the article's ranking probably does NOT transfer

It benchmarks an **iPhone 17 Pro (A19 Pro)** on **Gemma 4 E2B / Qwen 3.5 2B @ ~4-bit**, 128-token
generation. Its result: LiteRT-LM **55.4** tok/s · MLX-Swift **47.5** · llama.cpp **37.8** ·
CoreML/ANE **33.4**; peak RAM LiteRT **641 MB** · CoreML **1,187** · MLX **2,900** · llama.cpp **3,156**.

Three structural reasons that ordering may invert here:

1. **Memory is the binding constraint there and is not here.** The ranking rewards frugality — which
   is why CoreML and LiteRT top the RAM table. A 12B-4bit is **6.3 GB against 32 GB unified**.
   The axis their winners win on mostly stops mattering.
2. **The ANE/GPU balance inverts.** A19 Pro pairs a modest GPU with a strong ANE, so CoreML competes.
   **M1 Max has a 32-core GPU** and a comparatively weak 16-core ANE — MLX/Metal has far more
   headroom on desktop, and CoreML very likely cannot host a 12B at all.
3. **Scale differs ~6×**, and one cached model is a **26B-A4B MoE**. Runtime rankings are not known
   to survive either jump.

🔴 **Do NOT compare the article's 47.5 tok/s to wom7mlx's recorded ~28 tok/s.** Three confounds at
once: different chip, ~6× model size, and **the 28 was a multimodal (`mlx-vlm`) path, not text-only**.
That is the estate's signature defect — numbers that measure different things — and it is the
specific error this ticket exists to avoid repeating.
