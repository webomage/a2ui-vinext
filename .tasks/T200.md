## Why these four and not the other 32

**36 open tickets touch authorization** (measured: title + tags + epic + body over all 195 ticket
files). They do not all belong together. These four are the ones that share **the same three
artefacts**:

1. **The same tables.** `clients` · `contracts` · `client_users` · `blueprints` · `projects` ·
   `proposals` · `offices` · `office_capabilities` · `capabilities` · `user_roles` ·
   `user_active_role` · `project_acl` · `blueprint_acl`. Every one appears in at least two of the four.
2. **The same missing function.** There are **38 capabilities and zero readers** — no SQL function,
   no policy, no application code consults the catalog. T117 designs the model, T102 is the
   project-scoped grant it resolves against, T198's A1–A4 need a tenant boundary something reads, and
   T199 F2 governs the definer surface such a reader would live in. **A reader built for any one of
   them is the reader for all four**; four separately-built ones would be four holes through RLS.
3. **The same delivery path.** Every fix is DDL/RLS ⇒ a committed migration in
   `wom7api/supabase/migrations/` applied with `just db-migrate`. ⛔ **`db-migrate*` is denied to the
   agent ([[T161]] AC3), so every one of the four ends at the same owner action.** Four tickets = four
   owner round-trips for changes that touch overlapping policies on the same tables, with a real risk
   of a later migration reverting an earlier one's policy.

🔑 **The batch exists because of point 3.** Points 1 and 2 make it efficient; point 3 makes it
*correct*. Two of these tickets dropping a policy arm on `blueprints` in separate migrations is how a
fix gets silently undone.

here so it is visibly deferred rather than accidentally omitted.
